Honesty is difficult. At times, a moment of confronting honesty sparks a journey. Along the way, the echoes of that journey are picked up, swallowed, and then sung. Golden Bridge is such a journey.

A few years ago, when I returned to my hometown, I bought a pack of “Golden Bridge” cigarettes. The packaging featured a golden bridge with elegant script typeface. If placed in the dark, the bridge emitted a faint golden glow. I had never seen it anywhere else and mistakenly assumed it was produced in Guizhou. Since then, each time I traveled back home, I would take a few packs with me. Later, I discovered that Golden Bridge was actually produced in Fujian, and eventually, I realized it could be found almost everywhere: a certain bridge, a small town, a residential complex, a banquet hall, or an old shopping mall...

To me, Golden Bridge signifies a particular mark of growth for most of the post-90s generation. It’s worn, outdated, yet never truly disappeared. In the past, its recurring presence evoked no real wandering thoughts. But once childhood fades, if someone were to catch a glimpse of the gold gleaming through rust in the blazing sun, memories would stir. That flash of gold, renewed and vibrant, would bring forth whispers of wonder: “Remember then…” It was this kind of serendipity that gave me the chance—to immerse myself time and again in that golden hue. Over time, I was astonished to realize that this was an emotion: longing. In the end, I chose to face this feeling and relive it, stepping onto the golden bridge, carrying the last glow of my childhood toward the imagined shores beyond. The journey was not straightforward; to find and hold onto that feeling required focus. A slight distraction could cause me to lose it, and sometimes that golden hue was so sweet that it lulled me into a dreamy stupor.

Nostalgia takes many forms. I’ve always tried to find traces of the past in new experiences. Sometimes I find them, sometimes I don’t. More often than not, these echoes only live in old television sets or radio broadcasts. They are not from the golden moments I directly experienced, but mere fragments I’ve heard of, scattered impressions from a distant past. So, I create.

Amid the new crowds, on new streets, with new amusement rides and at new dining tables, I see it—a fleeting glimpse of time, slipping away.
